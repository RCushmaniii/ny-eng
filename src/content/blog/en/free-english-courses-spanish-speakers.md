Most free English courses teach you grammar rules. You memorize them. You understand them. And then your manager asks you a question in a meeting — and you freeze anyway.

The problem isn't the rules. The problem is that you've never heard what the right answer *sounds like* coming out of a fluent speaker's mouth. You can't copy a voice you've never heard.

That's what these courses fix.

## Real American English Audio — On Every Phrase

Every lesson in every course on this site is built with on-demand pronunciation audio. Not a robotic text-to-speech voice from 2010. The audio engine uses **Azure Neural TTS** — the same technology behind Microsoft's Immersive Reader, Adobe's voice assistant, and LinkedIn's learning platform. It sounds genuinely human: natural pace, natural rhythm, natural intonation.

Here's how it works: next to every key phrase or sentence in a lesson, you'll see a small speaker button. Click it. You hear a fluent American English speaker say it at natural speed. Click again. Repeat it. That's the loop — hear it, say it, hear it again.

Try it right now on this article. Click the speaker next to any of these:

- <span class="speak-en">I'd like to follow up on our previous conversation.</span>
- <span class="speak-en">Could you walk me through the timeline?</span>
- <span class="speak-en">Let me circle back to you on that.</span>

That's exactly what every lesson sounds like — business English at the pace and register you'll actually hear in a meeting.

In the course lessons, the audio goes further: full paragraph-length professional narratives, complete boardroom sentences, before-and-after comparisons of weak vs. polished phrasing. You're not just hearing words in isolation. You're hearing the cadence of someone who belongs in the room.

One more thing: if you're reading the Spanish version of an article or lesson, the speaker buttons are still there — and they still play American English. You can read in Spanish and hear the target language. No switching tabs, no separate app.

## Built Specifically for Spanish Speakers

These courses don't start from zero. They start from your Spanish.

You already know more than 1,500 English words through cognates — words that exist in both languages with the same meaning. Words like <span class="speak-en">professional</span>, <span class="speak-en">communication</span>, <span class="speak-en">executive</span>, <span class="speak-en">presentation</span>, <span class="speak-en">organization</span>. Most English courses ignore this and waste your first month teaching you things you already know.

These courses don't. Beginner learners activate what's already there. More advanced learners fix the specific interference patterns that Spanish creates in English: the tendency to translate word-for-word, the confusion between simple past and present perfect, the false cognates that embarrass you in formal settings, the moments where your brain stalls while it searches for the English equivalent.

Each course targets the exact gap Spanish speakers face at that level — not generic English problems, but the specific ones you're actually running into.

## Which Course Should You Start?

If English still feels completely new, start with **Beginner**. If you've covered the basics but can't yet talk about yesterday or make plans, go to **Elementary**. If you can already hold a conversation but you're still translating mentally, jump to **Intermediate**. If your English is fluent but you know it's not polished, go **Advanced**. If you're a senior leader who needs to command in English, go straight to **Executive** — and once you've worked through the frameworks, pair it with **Drive the Decision** to apply them to ten real boardroom scenarios. If past tenses are your specific freeze point regardless of your overall level, the **Past Tenses Master Class** runs alongside any of them — your progress is tracked separately per course.
