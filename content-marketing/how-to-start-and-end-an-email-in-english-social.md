## Facebook Post

"I remain attentive to your comments."

Perfectly correct English. And the clearest sign that an email was written in Spanish first. "Quedo atento" has no English twin, so the translation gives you away in the last line.

The first and last lines of an email decide how senior you sound. Most professionals translate both from Spanish: "Dear Sir" to someone they already know, "Greetings" as a sign-off, "Sorry for the inconvenience" on a routine request.

New on the blog: a copy-ready reference for business email in English. Greetings by situation, first lines, closing lines, sign-offs from most formal to most casual, subject lines, and one email rewritten before and after. Every phrase has audio.

## Twitter/X Thread

**Tweet 1 (Hook):**
"I remain attentive to your comments."

Grammatically fine.

Also the fastest way to tell a reader your email was written in Spanish first.

**Tweet 2:**
The first and last lines of an email carry more weight than everything in between.

The first line says whether you're a peer or asking permission.
The last line says what happens next.

**Tweet 3:**
How to start:

Hi Maria, for almost all business email.
Dear Ms. López, only for a formal first contact.

Then the reason in the first sentence. "Attached is the Q3 report." No warm-up.

**Tweet 4:**
How to end:

One line that names the next step. "Could you confirm the new dates by Thursday?"

Then a short sign-off. When in doubt: Best regards,

**Tweet 5:**
Translated habits to drop:

"I remain attentive" (quedo atento)
"Greetings," as a sign-off (saludos)
"Cordially," (cordialmente)
"Sorry for the inconvenience" on a routine request
