## The verdict, in one paragraph

NY English Teacher is not an acquisition problem to be solved with more traffic. It is a
**mix** problem. The teaching line already earns ~64,000 MXN/month from ~15 individual
students and three small invoiced accounts, and the operating posture since 2026-08-16 is to
*keep* that base while Robert's hours go to CushLabs. Corporate accounts are the right thing to
chase — not because they grow the line, but because **one account of five people is five
students with one relationship, one invoice, and near-zero churn**, which is the operating
vision ("larger revenue out of a smaller machine") applied to teaching. Every corporate
account that lands lets a low-rate individual relationship attrite without the income
following it. The channels that produce corporate accounts are, in order of proximity to
money: referrals from the accounts and students you already have, direct outreach to
Guadalajara operating leaders, and inbound via WhatsApp and the Google Business Profile.
Organic search is now *structurally able* to contribute for the first time, and it is a
six-month compounding bet, not a pipeline.

## The reframe: corporate is leverage, not growth

Three facts from the ICP and strategy files, put next to each other:

1. Every corporate account has arrived by referral. A director had a good experience, or one
   of their people did, and brought Robert in.
2. Robert has **never lost a corporate client after the demo class.** The close rate once a
   buyer is watching a real session is, on the record, 100%.
3. A corporate seat is company-funded, invoiced, and positioned as a perk. It does not ghost
   after the intro call the way ~90% of individual prospects do.

So the corporate line has a solved close and an unsolved *top of funnel*. The demo class is the
weapon; nothing feeds it. The whole strategy reduces to: **put more evaluators in front of a
demo class, at the lowest hour cost, without touching Robert's public identity.**

That last clause is not optional. The 2026-08-06 decision stands: Robert's person is committed
to CushLabs, so every NY English channel that routes through his name — LinkedIn presence,
HARO bylines, podcasts, speaking — is rejected. What remains is the business entity and
private, unindexed contact. That constraint eliminates most of what a marketing playbook would
suggest, and it is why the ranking below looks the way it does.

## The plan — 90 days

Sequenced so that the earliest items cost Robert the least and sit closest to money.
Effort estimates are in AI-agent time for the build and human time for Robert's part.

### Tier 1 — this week. Closest to money, lowest cost.

**1. The referral ask, as a system.**
Terramar's COO and Sourceability's contact are warm, invoiced, and already paying. Each knows
five peers. Ask them — once, well — for one introduction each to a peer whose team works in
English. Same ask to the strongest individual students, framed around *their* company.
- *Build:* a short email or WhatsApp message per contact via the `cold-email` skill (warm
  variant), plus a one-line tracker in this file: who was asked, when, outcome.
- *Robert:* sends five messages. Under an hour, once.
- *Closes when:* every current corporate contact and top-5 student has been asked once, and
  the outcome of each is written down here.
- *Why first:* it is the only channel with a demonstrated 100% close, and it has never been
  done deliberately.

**2. Refresh the GBP baseline and ask the corporate contacts for reviews.**
The register has carried "GMB stale since March" for five months. The profile is the best
channel the business has and nobody knows its current numbers. Two corporate reviews that
mention *team training* would do more for corporate credibility than any blog post — they
appear in the pack the April prospect found you through.
- *Robert:* reads three numbers off the GBP dashboard (reviews, rating, interactions) and
  sends two review requests. Thirty minutes.
- *Closes when:* `GMB-LOG.md` has a 2026-09 entry and the register item is gone.

### Tier 2 — next two weeks. Approved, unblocked, unbooked.

**3. Direct outreach to Guadalajara operating leaders — a sequence, not a blast.**
Target the pattern in the existing client base, not "HR in Guadalajara": mid-to-large
companies with a US parent, US customers, or a nearshore operation — logistics, med device,
manufacturing, IT services. Twenty to forty *named* people beats four hundred addresses.
Target the operating leader (COO, GM, plant/ops director) over HR; the accounts on record
came in through operators who felt the problem, not through L&D.
- *Build:* target list (LinkedIn for research only, never for the send), a 3-touch sequence
  via the `cold-email` skill leading to the demo class, sent from a `send.` subdomain or after
  the root-domain SPF question is settled. Ten to fifteen a day, not a hundred — the domain
  carries real transactional mail and is the one asset here that cannot be replaced.
- *Robert:* approves the list and the copy. Replies to responses. ~30 min/week.
- *Closes when:* first sequence sent to the first 20, and reply/demo/close counts recorded
  here weekly.
- *Deliverability caveats, recorded 2026-08-30:* root SPF is Brevo, Resend authenticates as
  `send.`; DMARC is `p=none`. Settle the From address before send one.

**4. Capture the learners the course pages already attract.**
327 impressions a quarter land on lesson pages and leave. One email capture on the course
hubs and cheat sheets — "get the next lesson" — builds the only list this business will have.
Not a corporate channel; it is the pool item 1 draws from in six months, and the cheapest
compounding asset on this list.
- *Build:* one component, one Neon table or Resend audience, one delivery email. ~2 hours.
- *Closes when:* a learner list exists and grows without Robert touching it.

### Tier 3 — the compounding bet. Front-load, then leave alone.

**5. The buyer-intent content cluster — six pieces, not fifty.**
The category page shipped this week will not hold rank alone against local schools with years
of topical depth. Six strong pieces written for the person who *pays for* someone else's
English, all linking to the category page and to each other:
- How to evaluate a corporate English provider in Mexico (the guide already exists as a PDF —
  publish it as a page)
- What HR receives from a structured program (baseline, monthly reviews, company report)
- Corporate English training costs in Mexico, honestly
- English training for logistics teams / for engineering teams (two pieces, both industries
  already in the client base)
- Why corporate English programs fail — already written; retitle and link it into the cluster
- *Build:* ~30–40 min each via `blog-post` + `copywriting`, EN + ES.
- *Closes when:* all six are live, interlinked, and the category page has entered the index
  (check with `gsc-index-status.mjs`, not by assumption).

**6. Measure corporate separately, forever.**
`scripts/seo/corporate-leads.mjs` now reads the only bot-proof corporate signal on the site.
Add a line to the tracker in this file every month: leads, demos, closes, by source. Pageviews
are not a corporate metric and never were — 68% of "visitors" are in Singapore.

## What not to do, and why

- **Do not chase `maestro de inglés` or any head term for corporate.** Wrong intent (cheap
  tutoring), wrong buyer (individuals), and the evidence that a head term is unwinnable at
  current authority is right there: `clases de inglés` sits at position 44 while its local
  variants rank #1 and produce nine impressions in six months.
- **Do not spend Robert's hours on NY English growth work.** The posture is maintain. Every
  item above is built by Claude and costs Robert minutes, or it is cut.
- **Do not judge any of this by the Vercel visitor count.** It measures crawlers.
- **Do not add paid acquisition before items 1–3 have produced a baseline.** A peso in with
  no instrumented peso out is a donation.
- **Do not reopen the LinkedIn / HARO / byline decision inside this file.** It is settled in
  `OFF-SITE-MARKETING.md` and the reasoning has not changed.

## Decisions only Robert can make

These block specific items above and nothing in a repo can answer them.

1. **What happened to the April prospect?** Four to six middle managers, inbound by WhatsApp,
   discovery call 2026-04-20, not on the current corporate roster. If it died, *why* is the
   most useful sentence in this whole plan — it is the only lost corporate deal on record.
2. **Which five contacts get the first referral ask** (item 1). Robert knows which
   relationships can carry it.
3. **Operating leader or HR** as the outreach target (item 3). The plan recommends operators.
4. **The From address** for outreach (item 3). `send.nyenglishteacher.com` authenticates
   through Resend today; the root domain authenticates through Brevo. One of them has to be
   the sender and it is not a casual choice.
5. ~~**Capacity ceiling.**~~ **Answered 2026-09-02.** Robert's calendar is already full. The
   target is **one more corporate account, ideally 2–3 students** — not the +5-seat account
   the plan modeled, and not a push for more than that. He is "not worried about it" either
   way, which reads as: this is upside to capture opportunistically, not a number to chase.
   **Consequence for the plan:** items 1–3 stay exactly as sequenced (referral asks, GBP
   refresh, outreach) — the target account size just shrinks the bar for "done." One
   qualifying close on any of those three channels satisfies this cycle. Do not scale outreach
   volume up to fill a bigger number than the one Robert actually wants.

## Confidence, stated the way the house rules require

- **That referral asks (item 1) produce at least one demo class within 60 days — 75%.** The
  close-after-demo rate is on record; the ask has never been made systematically; the
  remaining 25% is that warm contacts may not have a peer with a live need right now.
- **That outreach (item 3) produces at least one demo in 90 days — 55%.** Deliverability on a
  domain that has never sent cold volume is the named risk, and cold email to Mexican
  operating leaders is a slower-reply culture than the US playbooks assume.
- **That the category page ranks for its term within 90 days — 60%.** The SERP is soft; the
  page has no inbound links and no cluster behind it yet. Item 5 is what moves this number.
- **That this plan grows corporate share of teaching revenue from ~19% to ~35% within six
  months without adding net teaching hours — 50%.** That is the honest number for a plan whose
  first three items depend on humans replying. It is also the number that matters, because it
  is the mix change the operating vision asks for.
